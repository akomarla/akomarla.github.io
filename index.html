Aparna Komarla

Berkeley, California

Aparna Komarla is a data scientist and applied researcher working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, law, and social policy. Her work addresses a specific gap: sentencing reform laws pass, and then go largely unused, because the public defenders responsible for acting on them lack the data infrastructure to identify eligible cases and assemble the evidence those laws require. She builds the statistical and machine learning systems that close that gap.

She is the founder and lead data scientist of Redo.io, a California nonprofit that generates statistical evidence reports for claims under the California Racial Justice Act. The platform helps under-resourced defenders find incarcerated people serving excessive sentences, surface comparable cases, and document patterns of racial disparity in charging and sentencing in a form usable in court filings. Redo.io is supported by the Urban Institute and Microsoft Catalyst Grant Program, the Urban Justice Center Social Justice Accelerator, and LexLab at UC Law San Francisco, and works with Stanford Law's Three Strikes Project and California's Office of the State Public Defender.

Before Redo.io, she founded the Covid In Custody Project, which built the only comprehensive public dataset on COVID-19 across all 58 California county jails. Leading a team of more than 60 interns, she filed over 250 public records requests to document outbreaks, uncounted deaths in custody, and the refusal of many sheriffs to report data at all. That work was published in the Nevada Law Journal, covered in the San Francisco Chronicle, CalMatters, and the Sacramento Bee, and integrated into the UCLA Law COVID Behind Bars Data Project.

She is a Responsible AI Fellow at Duke University School of Law and a data scientist at Intel Foundry, where she builds forecasting models for semiconductor market strategy. Her research has appeared at ACM CHI, ACM FAccT, ICAIL, INFORMS, and APPAM.
